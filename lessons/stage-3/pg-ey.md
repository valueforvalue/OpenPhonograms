## New Learning: The Phonogram **ey**

<div class="phonogram">ey</div>

**ey** says 2 sounds: /ā/ /ē/

EY has two sounds: /ā/ (as in 'they') and /ē/ (as in 'key' or at the end of multi-syllable words like 'valley'). When unstressed at the end, it usually says /ē/.


| Sound | Example Words |
|-------|--------------|
| /ā/ | they, hey, prey, obey |
| /ē/ | key, valley, money, turkey |

---

## Spelling Analysis

| Word | Phonograms Used | Rules Applied | Say-to-Spell |
|------|----------------|---------------|--------------|
| they | th (/th/), ey (/ā/) | EY=/ā/ | /thā/ |
| key | k (/k/), ey (/ē/) | EY=/ē/ | /kē/ |
| valley | v (/v/), a (/ă/), l (/l/), l (/l/), ey (/ē/) | Y unstressed = /ē/ | văl-ē |

<details class="teacher-script">
<summary>📖 Phonogram ey — Teacher Script</summary>

> **Say:** "Today we are going to learn a new phonogram. A phonogram is a letter or group of letters that makes one or more sounds."

<div class="phonogram">ey</div>

> **Say:** "This is the phonogram **ey**." *(Point to the card.)* "It says /ā/ /ē/."

> **Say:** "Listen to me say its sounds: /ā/ /ē/. Now you say them."

> *(Child repeats.)*

> **Say:** "Good. Now let's write it. Watch me first." *(Demonstrate writing ey.)*

> **Say:** "Now you write **ey** three times. Say '/ā/ /ē/' each time you write it."

> *(Child writes on whiteboard. Check formation.)*

> **Say:** "Every time you see this phonogram, you will try each of its sounds until the word makes sense. Let's practice with some words."



> **Say:** "What did you learn today?" *(Child responds: "The phonogram ey — it says /ā/ /ē/.")*
