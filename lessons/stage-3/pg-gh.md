## New Learning: The Phonogram **gh**

<div class="phonogram">gh</div>

**gh** says 1 sound: /g/

GH at the BEGINNING of a word says /g/. This is different from GH at the end (which is often silent — Rule 28). Only a few words use GH at the start, and they all have a spooky feel! (ghost, ghastly, ghoul)


| Sound | Example Words |
|-------|--------------|
| /g/ | ghost, ghastly, ghetto |

## Spelling Analysis

| Word | Phonograms Used | Rules Applied | Say-to-Spell |
|------|----------------|---------------|--------------|
| ghost | gh (/g/), o (/ō/), s (/s/), t (/t/) | GH=/g/ at start | /gōst/ |
| ghastly | gh (/g/), a (/ă/), s (/s/), t (/t/), l (/l/), y (/ē/) | GH=/g/ at start | găst-lē |
| ghoul | gh (/g/), ou (/ü/), l (/l/) | GH=/g/ at start | /gül/ |

<summary>📖 Phonogram gh — Teacher Script (click to expand)</summary>

> **Say:** "Today we are going to learn a new phonogram. A phonogram is a letter or group of letters that makes one or more sounds."

<div class="phonogram">gh</div>

> **Say:** "This is the phonogram **gh**." *(Point to the card.)* "It says /g/."

> **Say:** "Listen to me say its sounds: /g/. Now you say them."

> *(Child repeats.)*

> **Say:** "Good. Now let's write it. Watch me first." *(Demonstrate writing gh.)*

> **Say:** "Now you write **gh** three times. Say '/g/' each time you write it."

> *(Child writes on whiteboard. Check formation.)*

> **Say:** "Every time you see this phonogram, you will try each of its sounds until the word makes sense. Let's practice with some words."



> **Say:** "What did you learn today?" *(Child responds: "The phonogram gh — it says /g/.")*
