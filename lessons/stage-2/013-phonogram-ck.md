## New Learning: The Phonogram **ck**

### The Phonogram **ck**

<div class="phonogram">ck</div>

**ck** says one sound: /k/

<span class="rule-badge">Rule 26</span> **CK is used only after a single vowel which says its short (first) sound.**

| Word | Why CK? |
|------|---------|
| back | Short /ă/ + /k/ → use CK |
| duck | Short /ŭ/ + /k/ → use CK |
| stick | Short /ĭ/ + /k/ → use CK |
| clock | Short /ŏ/ + /k/ → use CK |

> **Spelling aid:** "CK is the /k/ you use after a short vowel at the end of a one-syllable word."

### CK vs. K vs. C

- At the **end** of a one-syllable word after a **short vowel**: use **CK** (*back, duck, stick*)
- At the **end** after a long vowel or consonant: use **K** (*make, milk, ask*)
- At the **beginning**: use **C** before a/o/u (*cat, cot, cut*) or **K** before e/i/y (*kept, kit*)

> Write **ck** three times on your whiteboard while saying "/k/ — used after a short vowel."
