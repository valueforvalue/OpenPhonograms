First Lesson in 5 Minutes

← Back to full curriculum · Phonogram Chart →

You need

A small whiteboard + marker, or paper + pencil. That's it. No special materials required for your first lesson.

Step 1: Check where the child is (1 min)

Ask: "What sounds does the letter a make?"

If the child says all three — /ă/ (at), /ā/ (na/tion), /ä/ (father) — they know the phonogram. Move to step 2 with a new letter.

If they say only one, or none: teach it now.

Step 2: Teach one phonogram (2 min)

Write a on the whiteboard.

Say: "This is the phonogram a. It says three sounds: ă, ā, ä. Say them with me."

Child repeats the three sounds in order. Repeat 3 times. Child writes a while saying the sounds.

The golden rule

Always teach all sounds of a phonogram from the start. Never teach "a says /ă/" and add more sounds later. That creates confusion the child must unlearn.

Step 3: Play with sounds (1 min)

Say: "I'm going to say some sounds. You tell me the word."

Say slowly: /k/ ... /ă/ ... /t/

Child blends: "cat!"

Try: /s/ /ĭ/ /t/ → sit, /d/ /ŏ/ /g/ → dog, /r/ /ŭ/ /n/ → run

Then reverse: "Say 'cat.' What sounds do you hear?" Child segments: /k/ /ă/ /t/.

Step 4: Spell one word (1 min)

Say: "Now you're going to write a word. The word is cat. A cat is an animal that says meow."

Then: "What sounds do you hear in 'cat'?" (/k/ /ă/ /t/)

Child writes c (for /k/), then a (for /ă/), then t (for /t/).

Child reads back: "c says /k/, a says /ă/, t says /t/ — cat!"

What NOT to do
  • Don't say "this letter says…" — say "this phonogram says…"
  • Don't teach letter names first — teach sounds. Names come later.
  • Don't teach "sight words" as whole shapes — every word decodes.
  • Don't use pictures as the primary cue — the child must read the letters.
  • Don't skip the writing — writing locks the phonogram into memory.
After 5 lessons

The child should be flashing through a, d, g, c, o phonogram cards and saying all their sounds within 2 seconds. If not, stay on these 5 until they are solid. Add qu, s, t, i, p next.
