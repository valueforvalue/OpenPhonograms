Spelling Analysis Protocol

The core teaching routine — keep this beside you during every lesson · ← Spelling Rules · Word Lists →

Why this routine

Spelling Analysis bonds the spelling, pronunciation, and meaning of a word. Research shows improving spelling directly improves reading fluency (Ouellette et al., 2017). This is not a test — it's the instruction.

One-Syllable Words — 5 Steps

1. Hear & Say

The word is cat. A cat is an animal that says meow.

Child repeats: "cat."
2. Segment & Finger Spell

What sounds do you hear in "cat"?

Child segments: /k/ /ă/ /t/. You hold up fingers: 1 finger per sound — if sound uses multi-letter phonogram, hold up 2+ fingers together.
3. Write

Child writes the word while sounding it out. You watch — don't correct yet.

Then child re-segments while you write it on the board, sound by sound.
4. Analyze

What phonograms did we use? What rules apply?

Underline multi-letter phonograms. Mark silent E with arrow to vowel. Circle markings. Name any spelling rules.
5. Read

Read the word sound by sound, then blend it.

/k/ /ă/ /t/ → cat!

Multi-Syllable Words — Add Say-to-Spell

Say-to-Spell is how you preserve spelling information that normal pronunciation hides. Use it whenever a word contains schwa (the lazy /ŭ/ or /ĭ/ sound).
Say normally

The word is about. I am talking about reading.
Say-to-Spell

Pronounce every vowel as if stressed. Pause between syllables. Articulate double letters.

/ā/ — /bout/. Now segment.
Segment Child segments the say-to-spell version, not the normal pronunciation.
Write Child writes based on the say-to-spell segments.
Read normally After analyzing, read the word the normal way.

Correction Protocol — Frame as Puzzle, Not Error

Never: "That's wrong. It's spelled B-A-C-K."
Always: "You wrote 'bak.' Let's look together. The sound /k/ at the end — is there a rule about CK? Yes — CK after a single short vowel! 'Back' has short /ă/, so we use CK. Let's add the C."

The goal: the child discovers the correction through the rule, not through your authority. This builds independence.

Choosing Words — The 3-2-1 Rule

For each lesson, choose 3-5 words:

  • 3 words using the new phonogram/rule introduced today
  • 2 words reviewing phonograms/rules from the last 2 weeks
  • 1 word pulling in something from earlier (spaced retrieval)
