TermDefinition
Base word
A word to which a prefix or suffix can be added. Removing the prefix/suffix returns the base. help → helpful, unhelpful. The base word carries the core meaning.
Suffix, Prefix, Root, Spelling analysis
Controlled R /kənˈtroʊld ɑːr/
When the letter R follows a vowel, it "controls" the vowel's sound — preventing it from saying its long sound. car → /ăr/, not /ā/. her → /ĕr/, not /ē/. LoE teaches this explicitly instead of treating it as a sight word.
Phonogram (ar, er, ir, or, ur), Rule 31
Decodable
A word is decodable when a reader can sound it out using phonograms and rules they have already been taught. Decodable texts contain only words a reader can fully decode. Decodable ≠ levelled. LoE texts are decodable to phonograms taught by that lesson.
Phonogram, Warm-up, Reader
Grapheme /ˈɡræfiːm/
The written representation of a sound (a phoneme). One phoneme may have multiple graphemes: the long /ē/ sound is graphemed as ee, ea, e, y, ie, ei, ey, eigh. "Phonogram" is LoE's preferred term for grapheme.
Phonogram, Phoneme
High-frequency word
A word that appears very often in English text (the, of, was, said). LoE deliberately rejects the term "sight word" — every high-frequency word is decodable once the reader knows enough phonograms and rules. (See the taught as th + e, was as w + a + s.)
Decodable, Phonogram
Letter
A single character in the alphabet (a–z). A letter is not the same thing as a phonogram. The letter s is one letter but the phonogram s has TWO sounds (/s/ and /z/). The letter a has FOUR sounds in the phonogram a (/ă/, /ā/, /ä/, /ŭ/).
Phonogram, Single-letter phonogram
Morpheme /ˈmɔːrfiːm/
The smallest unit of meaning. A morpheme may be a whole word (cat), a prefix (un-), a suffix (-ful), or a root (tract). LoE introduces morphology in Stage 4 to unlock thousands of words through 25 Latin roots.
Root, Prefix, Suffix, Base word
Multi-letter phonogram
A phonogram written with more than one letter: sh, th, ck, ee, ng, ar, er, oi, oy, ai, ay, ... (49 multi-letter PGs in the curriculum). All multi-letter PGs are introduced in Stage 2+.
Phonogram, Single-letter phonogram
Open syllable
A syllable that ends in a vowel, which says its long sound. ba·by (ba = open, long a). no·body (no = open, long o). See Rule 4 (A E O U at End of Syllable).
Closed syllable, Rule 4, Vowel team
Closed syllable
A syllable that ends in one or more consonants. The vowel is "closed in" and says its short sound. kit·ten (kit = closed, short i). bas·ket (bas = closed, short a).
Open syllable, Vowel team
CVC / see-vee-see /
Short for Consonant-Vowel-Consonant — the simplest word pattern for early readers: one consonant sound, one short vowel, one consonant (c-a-t, d-o-g, s-u-n, h-o-p). Each letter makes exactly one sound. No digraphs, no silent letters. CVC words are the first words a child learns to blend and segment after mastering individual phonograms. In the curriculum, CVC blending starts at Stage 1 Lesson 6.
Closed syllable, Blend, Segment
Phoneme /ˈfoʊniːm/
The smallest unit of sound that distinguishes meaning. cat has three phonemes: /k/, /ă/, /t/. Changing any one creates a new word: /b/ /ă/ /t/ = bat. Phonemes are spoken; graphemes are written.
Grapheme, Phonogram
Phonogram
A written symbol (one OR more letters) that represents one or more sounds. sh is one phonogram (one sound). a is one phonogram (four sounds). The curriculum teaches 75 phonograms: 26 single-letter + 49 multi-letter.
Single-letter phonogram, Multi-letter phonogram, Grapheme
Prefix
A morpheme added to the beginning of a base word that changes its meaning: un-, re-, pre-, mis-, dis-. LoE teaches prefixes in Stage 4+ via Latin roots.
Suffix, Root, Morpheme
Root
The core morpheme carrying the primary meaning of a word, often from Latin or Greek. tract (pull, draw): at·tract, sub·tract, con·tract, trac·tion. 25 Latin roots unlock thousands of Stage 5 vocabulary words.
Prefix, Suffix, Morpheme
Rule (spelling rule)
A pattern that explains when a phonogram says a particular sound. Example: Rule 1: C softens to /s/ before E, I, Y explains why cent = /s/, not /k/. The curriculum teaches 31 numbered rules. Most multi-letter PGs have a governing rule.
Silent E, Phonogram
Say-to-spell
Saying a multi-syllable word in a special way — pausing between syllables, stressing every vowel — so the child can hear the spelling information that normal pronunciation hides. about normally /ə-BOUT/, say-to-spell /ā-BOUT/. Introduced with the word little in Stage 2.
Schwa, Say-to-spell (curriculum.md §Teaching Methodology)
Schwa /ʃwɑː/
The lazy, unstressed vowel sound /ŭ/ or /ĭ/ that appears in unstressed syllables of most multi-syllable English words. a·bout (a = schwa), lov·e (o = schwa), doc·tor (o = schwa). See Rule 31.
Say-to-spell, Rule 31
Silent E
A final e that is not pronounced but affects pronunciation or grammar. Rule 12 enumerates 9 reasons a Silent E appears: long vowel (make), C→/s/ (race), G→/j/ (cage), no plural (size), no V/U (have), TH voiced (house), look bigger (prize), unseen (come), clarifies (these). Each reason has its own sub-rule.
Rule 12, Vowel team
Single-letter phonogram
A phonogram written with exactly one letter (a–z, plus qu). All 27 single-letter PGs are taught in Stage 1.
Multi-letter phonogram, Phonogram
Spelling analysis
The 5-step teaching routine at the heart of every lesson: 1) Hear & Say, 2) Segment, 3) Write, 4) Analyze, 5) Read. The teacher says the word; the child segments it into sounds, writes it, identifies phonograms and rules, then reads it back. Spelling drives reading.
reference/spelling-analysis.html, Phonogram, Rule
Suffix
A morpheme added to the end of a base word that changes meaning or grammatical function: -ing, -ed, -ful, -less, -tion, -ly, -er. Spelling rules 13–16 govern common suffixing patterns (drop silent E, double consonant, y → i, no two i's adjacent).
Prefix, Base word, Rules 13–16
Syllable
A unit of pronunciation with one vowel sound. ba·by has two syllables; kit·ten has two. Every syllable needs at least one vowel (Rule 12.4). Syllable types: closed, open, vowel team, consonant-le, schwa.
Open syllable, Closed syllable, Schwa, Rule 12.4
Vowel team
Two or more vowels working together to make one sound. ea in eat, ai in rain, ou in cloud. The second vowel usually says its name; the first is silent.
Phonogram, Silent E, Open syllable
Warm-up
The word box before a decodable reader that pre-teaches target phonograms and key vocabulary using diacritical marks. The reader story itself is unmarked. Warm-ups are NOT in the story text.
Decodable, Diacritical marks, reference/diacritical-legend.html
What LoE rejects

"Sight word" — LoE rejects this term. Every word in English is decodable given enough phonograms and rules. Words like the, was, said, of are taught through their phonograms, not memorized as wholes. The curriculum uses "high-frequency word" instead, and each one is fully decodable by Stage 2+.

"Levelled reader" — LoE rejects this term. LoE readers are decodable, meaning every word can be sounded out using phonograms and rules taught before that lesson. A reader is "Stage 2 Lesson 14" decodable, not "level D" or "level 8."
