31 Spelling Rules

Scannable in under 2 minutes during a lesson · Rules grouped by teaching stage · ← Phonogram Chart · Spelling Analysis →

Stage 2 Rules (Kindergarten) Teach first

11. Q always needs a U; therefore, U is not a vowel here. queen, quit, quick
3. English words do not end in I, U, V, or J. Explains: have (not hav), blue (not blu), play (not plai), boy (not boi)
26. CK is used only after a single vowel which says its short (first) sound. back, stick, clock, duck
9. AY usually spells the sound /ā/ at the end of a base word. day, play, stay (vs. rain — AI never at end)
4. A E O U usually say their long sounds at the end of the syllable. ba·by, e·ven, go, u·nit
6. When a one-syllable word ends in a single-vowel Y, it always says /ī/. by, my, cry, fly, why
20. -ED, past tense ending, forms another syllable when the base word ends in /d/ or /t/. Otherwise, -ED says /d/ or /t/. want·ed (/ed/), played (/d/), fished (/t/)
30. We often double F, L, and S after a single, short or broad vowel at the end of a base word. (Floss Rule) off, bell, miss, buzz, egg

Stage 3 Rules (Grade 1)

12. Silent Final E — Nine Reasons
12.1 Vowel says long sound because of the E make, time, note
12.2 English words do not end in V or U have, blue, true
12.3 C says /s/ and G says /j/ because of the E dance, change
12.4 Every syllable must have a written vowel lit·tle, ta·ble
12.5 Keep singular S-words from looking plural house, goose
12.6 Make the word look bigger pie, rye, awe
12.7 TH says its voiced sound because of the E bathe (vs. bath)
12.8 Clarify meaning bye (vs. by), ore (vs. or)
12.9 Unseen reason (historical) come, some, done
1. C always softens to /s/ when followed by E, I, or Y. Otherwise, C says /k/. cent, city, cycle vs. cat, cot, cut
2. G may soften to /j/ only when followed by E, I, or Y. Otherwise, G says /g/. gem, giant, gym (BUT: get, give, girl — may, not always)
5. I and Y may say /ĭ/ or /ī/ at the end of a syllable. i·tem, bi·cy·cle, by
7. I and Y may say long /ē/
7.1 Y says /ē/ only in unstressed syllable at end of multi-syllable word ba·by, fun·ny
7.2 I may say /ē/ with silent E, at end of syllable, at end of foreign words ma·rine, ra·di·o
8. I and O may say /ī/ and /ō/ when followed by two consonants. find, kind, old, most
10. When a word ends with A, it says /ä/. A may also say /ä/ after a W or before an L. spa, wa·ter, ball, ma·ma
25. DGE is used only after a single vowel which says its short (first) sound. bridge, edge, judge
27. TCH is used only after a single vowel which says its short or broad sound. catch, watch, pitch
28. AUGH, EIGH, IGH, OUGH — GH phonograms used only at end of base word or before T. GH is either silent or says /f/. taught, eight, light, thought; cough, enough
31. Schwa — any vowel may say /ŭ/ or /ĭ/ in an unstressed syllable or word.
31.1 Any vowel → schwa in unstressed syllable a·bout, hap·pen
31.2 O may say /ŭ/ next to W, TH, M, N, V love, son, month
31.3 AR/OR may say /er/ in unstressed syllable dol·lar, col·or

Stage 4 Rules (Grade 2)

13. Drop the silent final E when adding a vowel suffix only if allowed by other spelling rules. make → making, hope → hoping (BUT: change → changeable — keep E to soften G)
14. Double the last consonant when adding a vowel suffix to words ending in one vowel + one consonant, only if the syllable before suffix is stressed. run → running, begin → beginning (BUT: open → opening)
15. Single-vowel Y changes to I when adding any ending, unless the ending begins with I. cry → cries, happy → happiness (BUT: cry → crying)
16. Two I's cannot be next to one another in English words. Explains why crying not criing, studying not studiing
17. TI, CI, and SI are used only at the beginning of any syllable after the first one. na·tion, spe·cial, ses·sion
18. SH spells /sh/ at the beginning of a base word and at the end of the syllable. SH never spells /sh/ at the beginning of any syllable after the first one, except for -ship. ship, fish, sun·shine, friend·ship
19. To make a verb past tense, add -ED unless it is an irregular verb.
21. Plural: add -S, unless the word hisses or changes; then add -ES. cats, dogs, dish → dishes, box → boxes
22. 3rd person singular verb: add -S, unless the word hisses or changes; then add -ES. runs, catches (only 4 irregular: has, does, goes, is)
23. AL- is a prefix written with one L when preceding another syllable. al·so, al·though, al·ways
24. -FUL is a suffix written with one L when added to another syllable. help·ful, hope·ful, thank·ful
29. Z, never S, spells /z/ at the beginning of a base word. zip, zoo, ze·bra (not sip for /zip/)
