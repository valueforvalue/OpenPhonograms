Troubleshooting Quick Reference

Diagnose and fix common problems in under a minute · ← High-Frequency Words

Child guesses words instead of decoding

Cause: Taught to use pictures, context, or first-letter guessing instead of sounding through the word.

Fix:
  1. Use a notched card — reveal one phonogram at a time. Child says each sound, then blends.
  2. Remove pictures from reading practice entirely.
  3. Use nonsense words (mip, fap, lub, strem) — forces decoding, can't be guessed.
  4. Praise effort at sounding out, not just getting the right answer.

Confuses b and d

Cause: Visual similarity — both are stick + circle. The brain needs a motor/kinesthetic anchor.

Fix:
  1. Teach handwriting strokes: b = start at top, drop down, bump forward. d = start like c, go up and down.
  2. Verbal cue: "b is bat-and-ball" (stick/bat first, then circle/ball). "d is doorknob-and-door" (circle/doorknob first, then stick/door).
  3. Write each 10× while saying the stroke sequence aloud. Multi-sensory over visual-only.

Phonogram recall is slow (>2 seconds)

Cause: Phonogram not yet in long-term memory. Needs more retrieval practice.

Fix: Flash the card 5× per day across the day — not all at once. Spaced retrieval builds storage strength. Track which phonograms lag and give them extra flashes. Write it in sand/salt while saying sounds.

Spells phonetically but misses the rule (e.g., "bak" for "back")

Cause: Knows the phonogram sounds but not the spelling rule that constrains which phonogram to use.

Fix: During Spelling Analysis step 4, ask: "Is there a rule about this sound at the end of a one-syllable word with a short vowel?" Guide to CK rule. Frame as discovery, not error. "You got the sounds right! Now let's check the rule."

Reads word correctly but can't spell it

Cause: Reading (decoding) is easier than spelling (encoding). Spelling requires retrieving the exact phonogram from multiple options.

Fix: This is normal. Spelling lags reading. Increase Spelling Analysis frequency. Use the 3-2-1 word selection rule: 3 new, 2 recent, 1 old. Say-to-spell for multi-syllable words.

Silent E confusion — adds E everywhere or nowhere

Cause: Was taught "silent E makes the vowel long" as the ONLY reason. Doesn't know the other 8 reasons.

Fix: Teach all 9 reasons explicitly. For each spelling analysis word with silent E, ask "Which reason?" Child names it. This builds the habit of identifying the specific reason rather than a vague "add E sometimes" instinct.

Resists / cries / says "I can't read"

Cause: Reading has become associated with failure. The tasks are too hard or the child has been asked to guess too often.

Fix: Drop back to a stage where the child succeeds easily. Do only phonogram flash and phonemic awareness games for a week. Rebuild confidence. Never push through tears — that wires reading = pain. Short sessions (10 min) beat long ones.

Older child (8+) still can't decode single-syllable words

Cause: Gaps in phonogram knowledge. Was likely taught sight-word method and never learned the code.

Fix: Test phonogram knowledge first. Most older struggling readers know letter names but not all sounds. Start at Stage 1 phonograms but move fast. They'll race through phonemic awareness (already developed) but need the phonogram-to-sound mapping. Explain: "You're not behind. You were never taught the code. Let's learn it now."
